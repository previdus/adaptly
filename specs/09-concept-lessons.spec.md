# Learn first: concept mini-lessons

Before the first question at a previously unseen skill/difficulty, offer a short lesson with a worked example that is different from the live question. Include a story hook, one small idea, narrated animated steps, a takeaway and a no-pressure optional check. Never give the live answer. The question clock and game sounds pause while a lesson is open. Skip is always available and never changes stars, level, mastery or independent-answer records.

Track explored/skipped lesson visits per learner and stable skill/level key separately from assessment. Existing attempted stages need not interrupt returning learners. Opening a lesson from a question is always available. Save the decision via existing family persistence. Leaving or switching lesson mode stops voice and video; keyboard focus remains in the dialog. Support fullscreen, phone layouts, captions and reduced motion.

Video recommendations match specific concepts, not merely the broad subject. Only use discovered publisher URLs, retain title/channel/source/review notes, and limit surfaced choices to two. No autoplay, no iframe until selected. Shared videos are not pushed repeatedly: mark already opened resources and keep them available by choice. Do not claim completion based on clicking or imply a video is watched. Provide external fallback if embedding fails. Coverage gaps stay explicit rather than substituting an irrelevant video.

Present the Video option first and select it on opening a lesson. Put the Story option second. Video playback still requires a separate deliberate selection; opening the tab must not autoplay a video.

Teaching rubric: plain concrete language, explain why a rule works, one goal at a time, small worked steps, no unearned mastery claims, correct quantities/units, supported worked solution, child-controlled pace and no forced viewing. Generated future topics require a matching local lesson and a curated resource review before release.
